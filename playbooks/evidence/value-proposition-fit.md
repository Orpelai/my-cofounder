# Problem

Teams overdescribe products and underdefine the customer. A disciplined match is needed between important customer progress and the specific ways an offer relieves pain or creates gain.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Is one customer segment clearly defined?
- Are its jobs, pains, and gains evidenced and ranked?
- Does each offer element map to a priority item?
- Are irrelevant features excluded from the value story?
- Has the value hypothesis produced costly behavior?

Proceed when the profile or fit links are uncertain.

# Required inputs

- One coherent segment and buying/use context.
- Interview, observation, usage, support, win/loss, and transaction evidence.
- Customer jobs, pains, gains, current alternatives, and constraints.
- Product/service capabilities and delivery limitations.
- Decision, target metric, test budget, and deadline.

# Roles

- **Profile owner:** customer researcher.
- **Value-map owner:** product or offer owner.
- **Commercial owner:** validates buyer, value, and willingness to pay.
- **Evidence challenger:** tests ranking, causality, and unsupported claims.
- **Decider:** selects fit hypotheses and investment gate.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define one segment; separate user, buyer, and beneficiary when needed.
2. Build the **customer profile** from evidence:
3. List jobs to be done in the relevant circumstances.
4. List pains before, during, and after attempting those jobs.
5. List required, expected, desired, and unexpected gains.
6. Rank jobs, pains, and gains by frequency, severity, urgency, and consequence.
7. **Gate 1 — profile evidence:** stop if rankings come only from internal opinion.
8. Build the **value map**:
9. List products and services actually in scope.
10. State exactly how each element relieves a priority pain.
11. State exactly how each element creates a priority gain.
12. Remove mappings that are vague, low-priority, or operationally impossible.
13. **Gate 2 — fit hypothesis:** require direct links to the highest-priority profile items.
14. Write the riskiest assumptions about desirability, credibility, usability, price, and delivery.
15. Select the cheapest behaviorally valid test: prototype task, paid pilot, preorder, or live offer.
16. Predeclare success, harm, and inconclusive thresholds.
17. Run tests with the intended segment and capture contradictory evidence.
18. **Gate 3 — demonstrated fit:** distinguish problem-solution evidence from repeatable product-market fit.
19. Update both profile and map; preserve version history and decision rationale.

# Output contract

- Segment-specific Customer Profile and Value Map.
- Ranked jobs, pains, and gains with sources/confidence.
- Traceable offer-element → pain/gain links.
- Assumption and evidence ledger.
- Experiment contract and observed results.
- Decision: iterate, narrow, pivot, scale-test, or stop.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Paid conversion, repeat use, retention, customer outcome, willingness to pay, and contribution margin.

## Leading

- Problem frequency/severity and commitment rate.
- Target-job completion and correct understanding/belief of priority value claims.

## Guardrail

- Refunds, support burden, adverse outcomes, privacy, and expectation mismatch.

# Failure modes

- Mixing customer segments into one profile.
- Treating every customer request as equally important.
- Mapping features to generic words such as “easy” or “better.”
- Testing stated preference without commitment.
- Declaring product-market fit from one successful pilot.
- Ignoring the payer or delivery economics.

# Guardrails

- Keep evidence provenance and freshness visible.
- Separate customer fact, interpretation, hypothesis, and recommendation.
- Do not make health, financial, legal, or performance claims without substantiation.
- Protect vulnerable users and avoid manufacturing urgency.
- Revalidate when the segment, offer, price, or context changes.

# Adaptation notes

The Value Proposition Canvas is an originator/vendor framework. It provides a shared representation of fit hypotheses; it does not prove demand, satisfaction, or product-market fit.

For a small venture, test only the top one or two pains/gains first. Require a progression from interview evidence to behavior, payment, repeat use, and viable delivery.
