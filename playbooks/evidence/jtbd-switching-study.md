# Problem

Products are selected in circumstances, not because demographics mechanically cause purchase. The business needs to understand the progress people seek and the forces moving them toward or away from change.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Can the team reconstruct what triggered recent buying decisions?
- Is the prior solution or habit known?
- Are anxieties and adoption friction visible?
- Are functional, social, and emotional dimensions separated?
- Do segments predict different behavior rather than merely describe people?

Run the study when the decision timeline and forces remain unclear.

# Required inputs

- A specific decision: adoption, upgrade, switch, churn, or no-decision.
- 8–15 recent participants across successful and failed switches.
- Known dates, artifacts, usage, and commercial records where available.
- Neutral interview guide and consent/privacy plan.
- Decision the research will inform.

# Roles

- **Research lead:** conducts chronology-based interviews.
- **Observer:** records language, evidence, and contradictions.
- **Domain owner:** clarifies workflow without leading.
- **Synthesizer:** builds timelines, force maps, and circumstance clusters.
- **Decider:** selects product or go-to-market implications.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define the switch and recruit people close enough to the event for useful recall.
2. Include varied outcomes: adopted, abandoned, delayed, churned, and no-decision.
3. Begin with the first moment the old situation felt inadequate.
4. Reconstruct the timeline: first thought → passive looking → active search → decision → first use.
5. Ask what changed in the person’s circumstances, not what features they “wanted.”
6. Identify the prior solution, workaround, habit, or nonconsumption.
7. Capture **push** from the old situation and **pull** toward the new solution.
8. Capture **anxiety** about the new solution and **habit/inertia** preserving the old one.
9. Explore functional, social, and emotional progress in the same context.
10. **Gate 1 — concrete history:** reject generalized opinions unsupported by a specific event.
11. Seek corroborating artifacts: messages, searches, invoices, calendars, setup work, or usage.
12. Build one switching timeline and forces map per participant.
13. Cluster cases by circumstances and progress sought, not demographics alone.
14. **Gate 2 — repeatability:** require repeated patterns plus explicit exceptions.
15. Draft job statements describing situation, desired progress, and success.
16. Translate forces into product, onboarding, proof, channel, and messaging hypotheses.
17. Test those hypotheses with behavior: comprehension, trial, switching, retention, or payment.
18. **Gate 3 — predictive usefulness:** keep segments only if they improve decisions or forecast behavior.
19. Record what remains unknown and when to revisit.

# Output contract

- Participant switching timelines with evidence status.
- Forces-of-progress maps: push, pull, anxiety, and habit.
- Functional, social, and emotional dimensions.
- Circumstance-based job clusters and disconfirming cases.
- Product/GTM hypotheses linked to observed forces.
- Validation plan with behavioral thresholds.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Improved switching, activation, retention/churn, and qualification for the target circumstance.

## Leading

- Corroborated recent timelines and repeated forces across independent cases.
- Job-based hypotheses tested in market or product behavior.

## Guardrail

- Misclassification, refunds, expectation mismatch, or exclusion of valuable users.
- Recall quality and sample concentration.

# Failure modes

- Asking abstract “why” questions and accepting post-rationalization.
- Treating a desired feature as the job.
- Ignoring the old habit, no-decision, or nonconsumption.
- Reducing the job to functional utility.
- Segmenting by age or title after collecting circumstance evidence.

# Guardrails

- Protect participant identity and sensitive context.
- Separate participant words from researcher interpretation.
- Preserve cases that contradict the dominant story.
- Do not manipulate anxieties or exploit vulnerable circumstances.
- Validate recommendations with observed behavior and outcome metrics.

# Sources

- Christensen Institute, [Jobs to Be Done Theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/).

# Adaptation notes

The source defines JTBD as a lens on circumstances and functional, social, and emotional progress. The switching-study procedure is a practitioner adaptation and should not be attributed wholesale to the cited page.

Use the resulting jobs as operating hypotheses. They become useful only when they improve product, positioning, adoption, retention, or another predeclared decision metric.
