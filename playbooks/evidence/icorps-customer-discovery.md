# Problem

Technical possibility is not market evidence. Teams need a disciplined way to expose assumptions, learn outside the building, and reduce risk before scaling development or commercialization.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Can the team distinguish user, buyer, beneficiary, and decision maker?
- Is the priority problem supported by observed behavior?
- Is the current alternative known?
- Has anyone made a costly commitment?
- Are channel, pricing, and delivery assumptions tested?

Run discovery when three or more answers are “no” or low-confidence.

# Required inputs

- Technology/offer description and current readiness.
- Initial Business Model Canvas or equivalent hypothesis map.
- Target segments and stakeholder roles.
- Assumption register ranked by impact and uncertainty.
- Interview access plan and ethical/privacy constraints.
- Decision to inform, deadline, and evidence threshold.

# Roles

- **Entrepreneurial lead:** owns interviews and decisions.
- **Technical lead:** explains constraints without turning interviews into demos.
- **Mentor/challenger:** reviews evidence quality and bias.
- **Evidence steward:** captures notes, patterns, contradictions, and model changes.
- **Decider:** approves pivot, persevere, pause, or stop.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Frame one commercialization decision and its deadline.
2. Write explicit hypotheses for customer, problem, current alternative, value, channel, revenue, and delivery.
3. Rank assumptions by impact × uncertainty; select the riskiest current assumption.
4. Define interview cohorts across user, buyer, influencer, and channel roles.
5. Write a neutral guide about past behavior, context, workflow, consequences, and decisions.
6. **Gate 1 — research readiness:** reject leading questions, pitches, hypotheticals, and undefined samples.
7. Recruit outside the immediate network; track selection bias.
8. Conduct interviews in pairs when possible: one interviewer, one note taker.
9. Ask for recent specific examples, artifacts, spending, workarounds, and decision history.
10. Capture direct observations separately from interpretation.
11. Debrief after each interview; update facts, surprises, contradictions, and follow-ups.
12. Every 5–10 interviews, cluster repeated patterns by circumstances and stakeholder role.
13. **Gate 2 — pattern strength:** do not call a pattern validated without repeated, independent behavioral evidence.
14. Update the business-model hypotheses and document what changed and why.
15. Design the next cohort to challenge the emerging conclusion, not merely confirm it.
16. Seek stronger evidence: introduction, data access, pilot, preorder, payment, or implementation effort.
17. **Gate 3 — commitment:** distinguish stated interest from costly behavior.
18. At sprint end, decide: persevere, narrow, pivot, pause, or stop.
19. Convert unresolved high-impact assumptions into experiments.

# Output contract

- Versioned hypothesis/business-model map.
- Interview log with cohort, role, date, source, and consent constraints.
- Evidence table separating fact, quote, inference, and hypothesis.
- Repeated patterns plus disconfirming cases.
- Commitment evidence and its cost to the customer.
- Decision memo with next test, owner, threshold, and date.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Reduced high-impact uncertainty and stronger behavioral commitments supporting a commercialization decision.

## Leading

- Relevant interviews by role and assumptions/model changes backed by evidence.
- Disconfirming cases actively investigated.

## Guardrail

- Complaints, privacy violations, misleading promises, and sample concentration.
- Product work started before the evidence gate.

# Failure modes

- Counting interviews instead of learning quality.
- Asking whether people “would use” or “would pay.”
- Pitching before understanding current behavior.
- Averaging conflicting segments into one persona.
- Declaring validation from recurring words without consequences or action.

# Guardrails

- Obtain appropriate consent and minimize personal data.
- Never fabricate customer evidence or remove contradictory observations.
- Label sample limits/nonresponse bias; never promise product, price, timing, or confidentiality without authority.
- Require behavioral evidence before major irreversible investment.

# Sources

# Adaptation notes

NSF I-Corps is an immersive seven-week experiential commercialization program, not a generic interview certification. This shorter sprint adapts its “outside the laboratory” learning logic and must not be represented as completion of the NSF program.

Interviews reduce uncertainty; they do not establish market size, willingness to pay, or causal demand by themselves. Pair discovery with paid tests, usage, economics, and delivery evidence.
