# Problem

Teams can build coherent solutions to weak problems. Working Backwards forces a customer-first future state and exposes why the idea may not be compelling, feasible, or worth funding.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Can the customer and problem be stated specifically?
- Is the future experience meaningfully better, not merely feature-rich?
- Are adoption, economics, dependencies, and risks explicit?
- Can leaders explain “why now” and “why this solution”?
- Is there evidence strong enough for the next investment level?

Use PR/FAQ when the idea is material and several answers remain unclear.

# Required inputs

- Target customer, current alternative, problem evidence, and intended outcome.
- Strategic goal and investment decision.
- Product, technology, operations, legal, security, and financial constraints.
- Market and competitor evidence.
- Named author, reviewers, decider, and review date.

# Roles

- **Author/idea owner:** writes and revises the narrative.
- **Customer representative:** challenges customer truth and value.
- **Cross-functional reviewers:** product, technology, operations, finance, legal/risk.
- **Meeting facilitator:** protects silent reading and balanced participation.
- **Decider:** chooses go, rework, hold, or stop.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Write the future-dated press release from the target customer’s perspective.
2. Keep the PR under one page: customer, problem, solution, benefit, proof, and call to action.
3. Apply the “so what?” test: why is the experience meaningfully better, easier, faster, or cheaper?
4. **Gate 1 — compelling experience:** stop if value is generic, internally framed, or incremental without significance.
5. Write the FAQ, ideally no more than five pages.
6. Cover customer questions: use, switching, trust, accessibility, support, and failure.
7. Cover business questions: segment, alternatives, pricing, economics, adoption, and strategic fit.
8. Cover execution questions: architecture, operations, capacity, dependencies, security, legal, and launch.
9. Separate known facts, estimates, assumptions, and unresolved questions.
10. **Gate 2 — answerability:** require owners and tests for every high-impact unknown.
11. Circulate the document for a scheduled review.
12. Begin with silent reading; collect independent high-level reactions before detailed discussion.
13. Have senior participants speak last to reduce anchoring.
14. Review line by line; record omissions, disagreements, and evidence requests.
15. Revise repeatedly; use brevity as a forcing function for decisions.
16. **Gate 3 — investment readiness:** compare evidence, upside, cost, risk, reversibility, and alternatives.
17. Decide: greenlight next stage, rework, hold for a condition, or stop.
18. Keep the PR/FAQ living after approval; changes require visible rationale and review.

# Output contract

- Future-dated PR of one page or less.
- FAQ of approximately five pages or less.
- Assumption/evidence register and unresolved-question owners.
- Review minutes with independent objections and decisions.
- Investment decision, scope, budget, milestones, and stop conditions.
- Version history linking revisions to evidence.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Customer/business outcome stated in the PR and realized value versus forecast.

## Leading

- Critical unknowns resolved before build.
- Customer commitments or behavioral tests supporting the experience.
- Review cycles completed and high-risk objections closed.

## Guardrail

- Cost, schedule, reliability, security, privacy, accessibility, and customer harm.

# Failure modes

- Writing promotional copy full of unsupported claims.
- Starting from the desired technology or feature.
- Hiding uncertainty in appendices or vague language.
- Letting the most senior reviewer anchor discussion.
- Treating approval as permanent or the document as finished.
- Rewarding writing polish over evidence and viability.

# Guardrails

- Never fabricate customer quotes, adoption, economics, or proof.
- Mark forecasts and confidence explicitly.
- Require domain review for legal, privacy, security, safety, and finance.
- Avoid external publication until claims are verified and approved.
- Stop when the customer benefit cannot justify cost and risk.

# Adaptation notes

Working Backwards is an Amazon-originated practitioner process. Its documented use at Amazon does not prove equal performance in every organization; treat the PR/FAQ as an operating hypothesis and evaluate decision quality and launch outcomes.

For small decisions, use a one-page mini PR/FAQ. Preserve the same customer-value, evidence, risk, and decision gates.
