# Problem
A stalled company often responds with more initiatives. That makes diagnosis harder,
fragments ownership, and hides contradictory strategic beliefs. The reset room creates
a time-boxed evidence process that identifies the constraint, compares coherent choices,
and closes one strategy with measurable tests.

# Trigger diagnostic
- Has the primary outcome failed to improve for two or more meaningful periods?
- Are teams optimizing different definitions of success?
- Are customer, product, channel, and economic explanations in conflict?
- Can no one name the one decision that would remove the most uncertainty?
- Are more than three strategic initiatives competing for the same capacity?

# Required inputs
- Active goal, baseline, target, deadline, and scorecard.
- Customer, usage, sales, delivery, and cash evidence.
- Current strategy, initiatives, capabilities, and explicit commitments.
- Decision history and rejected alternatives.
- External changes, dependencies, and material risks.
- People with authority to stop work and reallocate capacity.

# Roles
- **D:** one decision owner for the reset.
- **R:** strategy lead who integrates evidence and options.
- **Evidence owners:** customer, product, revenue, operations, and finance.
- **Red team:** challenges causality, feasibility, and hidden risk.
- **P:** owners of the selected 90-day strategic tests.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Freeze new strategic initiatives for the duration of the reset.
2. Frame the outcome gap and review horizon in measurable terms.
3. Build a shared fact base; label facts, estimates, hypotheses, and unknowns.
4. Map the current system from customer demand through delivery and cash.
5. Identify candidate constraints and evidence that could disconfirm each.

**Gate 1 — diagnosis:** do not design strategy options until the group agrees on the
outcome gap, evidence quality, and top two plausible constraints.

6. Generate two to four coherent strategic choices, each with explicit exclusions.
7. For each option state customer, advantage, capabilities, economics, and sequence.
8. Run a premortem and risk assessment on each option.
9. Estimate expected upside, time to evidence, cost, reversibility, and downside.
10. Write a decision memo comparing options against shared criteria.

**Gate 2 — choice:** the D selects one strategy or requests a bounded evidence sprint.
Consensus is not required; rationale and rejected evidence are recorded.

11. Convert the choice into no more than three 90-day tests.
12. Stop or pause work inconsistent with the selected strategy.
13. Assign leading indicators, guardrails, owners, and weekly review.
14. Revisit the strategy only at the decision date or on a predeclared stop condition.

# Output contract
- Verified outcome-gap statement.
- Shared fact base and uncertainty register.
- Constraint diagnosis with disconfirming tests.
- Two to four coherent options and explicit exclusions.
- Decision memo and RAPID map.
- 90-day test portfolio, stopped-work list, and review cadence.

# Metrics
- Primary outcome gap and trend.
- Time from reset start to strategic decision.
- Percentage of capacity aligned to the chosen strategy.
- Leading evidence produced by each 90-day test.
- Number of conflicting initiatives stopped.
- Risk and cash guardrails.

# Failure modes
- Rebranding the existing plan without changing choices.
- Selecting a compromise bundle of incompatible options.
- Letting loud anecdotes outrank verified evidence.
- Confusing a symptom with the governing constraint.
- Keeping every old initiative “just in case.”
- Reopening the decision without new evidence.

# Guardrails
- The designated business owner or governance body retains final authority over the strategic goal and irreversible commitments.
- Do not manufacture certainty where data is sparse.
- Protect existing customers and critical obligations during the reset.
- External commitments, spending, or layoffs require their normal approvals.
- Preserve dissent, rejected options, and comparability of historical metrics.

# Adaptation notes

Use a one-day reset for a solo business and a multi-session room for cross-functional
organizations. The synthesis is valuable only if it ends in stopped work, bounded tests,
and a single accountable decision—not a larger strategy document.
