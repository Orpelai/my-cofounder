# Problem
Revenue is a connected flow. Increasing leads will not fix poor positioning, bad-fit
qualification, weak discovery, missing follow-up, unacceptable terms, or value that
fails to retain customers. Repair starts by locating the constraint with cohort evidence,
then changing one part of the system without hiding downstream harm.

# Trigger diagnostic
- Is the gap primarily reach, response, qualification, progression, close, activation, or retention?
- Are stage definitions and denominators stable?
- Which segment and channel produce the highest-quality customers?
- Why do qualified deals stall or choose no decision?
- Are terms and pricing aligned with delivered value and delivery cost?
- Does closed revenue become realized value, cash, and retention?

# Required inputs
- Revenue target, baseline, period, and cash definition.
- Funnel stages with cohort counts, age, and source.
- Segment, channel, offer, pricing, and margin data.
- Win, loss, no-decision, and churn evidence.
- Follow-up activity and next-step quality.
- Delivery capacity, activation, retention, refund, and reputation guardrails.

# Roles
- **Revenue owner:** owns the end-to-end outcome.
- **Demand owner:** owns qualified attention and channel evidence.
- **Sales owner:** owns discovery, progression, and follow-up.
- **Offer owner:** owns positioning, packaging, and terms.
- **Delivery owner:** validates capacity, value realization, and retention.
- **Analyst/red team:** checks definitions, cohorts, and alternative causes.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define realized revenue and the full customer journey.
2. Reconcile stage counts and cohort dates against source records.
3. Segment by customer fit, channel, offer, seller, and time.
4. Quantify flow, delay, leakage, and economics at each transition.
5. Review actual customer language from wins, losses, no-decision, and churn.

**Gate 1 — constraint:** choose one binding constraint only when evidence shows that
improving it can move revenue without immediately shifting failure downstream.

6. Form two or three causal hypotheses for the constraint.
7. Select the fastest reversible intervention with measurable downstream effects.
8. Define primary, leading, and guardrail measures.
9. If terms are negotiated, set BATNA and deal boundaries before concessions.
10. Execute for one defined cohort while maintaining comparison data.

**Gate 2 — scale:** expand only when the intervention improves realized revenue or a
credible leading indicator without degrading fit, margin, retention, or delivery.

11. Standardize the winning behavior in acquisition, follow-up, or content routines.
12. Run an after-action review on unexpected wins and losses.
13. Recalculate the constraint at the weekly business review.

# Output contract
- Reconciled revenue-system map.
- Constraint diagnosis and competing hypotheses.
- Cohort experiment with owner and decision date.
- Customer-language evidence and updated message or process.
- Scale, revise, or stop decision with downstream guardrails.

# Metrics
- Qualified demand and response rate.
- Stage conversion and stage age.
- No-decision and loss reasons.
- Sales-cycle duration and next-step adherence.
- Realized revenue, gross margin, and cash collection.
- Activation, retention, expansion, refund, and churn.

# Failure modes
- Buying more leads before fixing conversion.
- Optimizing meetings booked regardless of fit.
- Discounting without a deal boundary or reciprocal value.
- Treating all channels and segments as comparable.
- Celebrating signed contracts that do not activate or pay.
- Changing multiple funnel stages and losing causal clarity.

# Guardrails

- No deceptive scarcity, fabricated proof, spam, or hidden pricing.
- Protect customer fit, consent, deliverability, and brand reputation.
- Do not exceed delivery capacity to create short-term bookings.
- Preserve cohort definitions and historical funnel observations.
- External outreach, publishing, and commercial commitments require authorization.

# Adaptation notes

For low-volume founder-led sales, use case-by-case evidence and stage age rather than
false statistical precision. For higher volume, use cohorts and controlled changes.
Always diagnose the system again after an intervention; constraints move.
