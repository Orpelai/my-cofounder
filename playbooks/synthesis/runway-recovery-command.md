# Original composition notice
This is an original cross-domain composition for a general business operating system.
It is an operating response pattern, not financial, legal, tax, or insolvency advice.

# Problem
Runway crises worsen when profit, booked revenue, cash, and available credit are confused.
Teams make isolated cuts, preserve sunk costs, or chase low-quality revenue. Recovery
requires one verified cash forecast, explicit authority, staged actions, and protection
of the capabilities that create future cash.

# Trigger diagnostic
- What is unrestricted cash now and how was it reconciled?
- When does cash cross warning, intervention, and minimum-reserve thresholds?
- Which receipts, costs, and commitments drive the downside case?
- Which actions improve cash soon enough to matter?
- Which cuts would destroy customer value or the recovery engine?
- What legal, tax, payroll, creditor, or director duties apply?

# Required inputs
- Current cash and near-cash balances with reconciliation date.
- Rolling weekly inflows, outflows, commitments, and scenarios.
- Accounts receivable, payment timing, debt, taxes, payroll, and vendor obligations.
- Unit economics and contribution margin by offer or segment.
- Contractual termination rights and operational dependencies.
- Authority limits and professional advisers.

# Roles
- **D:** owner with authority over recovery choices.
- **Cash owner/R:** maintains forecast and recommendation.
- **Revenue owner:** owns collections and near-term contribution.
- **Cost owners:** validate timing, reversibility, and consequence of reductions.
- **Risk/legal advisers:** assess duties and prohibited actions.
- **P owners:** execute each approved cash action.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Reconcile cash, obligations, and forecast sources.
2. Build base, downside, and severe-downside weekly scenarios.
3. Set warning, intervention, minimum-reserve, and stop thresholds.
4. Freeze new nonessential commitments while the picture is validated.
5. Identify the few variables that dominate cash timing.

**Gate 1 — professional escalation:** if obligations may not be met, immediately seek
qualified legal, accounting, tax, or insolvency guidance before discretionary transfers.

6. Generate actions across collections, pricing, terms, scope, costs, financing, and timing.
7. Score each action by cash amount, arrival date, probability, cost, reversibility,
   customer harm, capability loss, and legal risk.
8. Premortem the combined recovery plan and register material risks.
9. Assign RAPID rights and daily or weekly evidence checkpoints.
10. Negotiate material terms using explicit BATNAs and approval boundaries.

**Gate 2 — execute:** approve a portfolio only when projected cash remains above the
minimum reserve under a declared scenario, or the shortfall and escalation are explicit.

11. Execute in reversible stages and compare actuals with forecast.
12. Protect high-contribution customers, critical delivery, and recovery capability.
13. Reforecast after every material variance and conduct an after-action review on exit.

# Output contract
- Reconciled weekly cash forecast with scenarios.
- Thresholds, authority map, and escalation conditions.
- Ranked action portfolio with cash timing and side effects.
- Protected-capability and stopped-commitment lists.
- Actual-versus-forecast log and next decision date.

# Metrics
- Unrestricted cash and downside runway.
- Weekly net cash flow and burn.
- Forecast error by major line.
- Collections amount, age, and probability.
- Contribution margin and cash conversion.
- Committed versus discretionary outflow.
- Customer, quality, and key-capability guardrails.

# Failure modes
- Treating booked revenue as available cash.
- Cutting evenly instead of protecting the constraint-solving capability.
- Assuming collections will arrive without owner-level evidence.
- Taking bad financing or customer terms under pressure.
- Hiding downside scenarios to avoid escalation.
- Measuring runway without taxes, payroll, or committed costs.

# Guardrails
- The designated business owner or governance body approves financing, layoffs, material vendor changes, and external commitments.
- Do not delay legally protected payments or misrepresent financial position.
- Preserve an audit trail and original forecast assumptions.
- Use qualified professionals when duties or insolvency risk may arise.
- Optimize survival with long-term value, not cash timing alone.

# Adaptation notes
For a solo business, a 13-week weekly model and one daily cash view may suffice.
For larger companies, separate treasury, board, creditor, and employee communication
workstreams. Thresholds must be chosen from real obligations and risk tolerance.
