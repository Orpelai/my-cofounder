# Live Event Fill Sprint

## Problem

A fixed deadline makes slow brand-only activity insufficient, while uncoordinated urgency creates spam, false scarcity, bad-fit attendees, and unmeasured effort.

## Trigger diagnostic

Confirm:

- exact target, baseline, deadline, capacity, and payment definition;
- offer, audience, price, venue, schedule, CTA, and registration flow;
- previous attendee/referral assets;
- current funnel and channel data;
- remaining delivery and support capacity.

If the CTA or checkout is broken, fix or replace it before scaling traffic.

## Required inputs

- Verified event facts: date, time, venue, capacity, price, and delivery owner.
- Confirmed-registration baseline, remaining gap, deadline, and source of truth.
- Approved payment/registration path or an approved conversational CTA.
- Segmented warm-prospect, prior-attendee/referrer, and partner lists.
- Channel-level tracking fields and explicit outreach authorization.

## Roles

- Decider: business owner or designated decision-maker
- Campaign manager: campaign lead
- Revenue manager: revenue lead
- Direct outreach: outreach lead and authorized contributors
- Content: content lead
- Distribution: distribution or partnerships lead
- Metrics: measurement lead
- Offer/fact gate: offer owner or designated fact checker

## Procedure

### Gate 1 — Fact and conversion readiness

Create a one-page fact sheet. Click through the CTA from a clean session. Confirm tracking and payment. If unavailable, use a truthful conversational CTA such as “reply with the event keyword” while the link is repaired.

### Gate 2 — Segment the remaining gap

Allocate the registration gap across:

1. qualified warm/direct prospects;
2. prior attendees and trusted referrers;
3. partners and communities;
4. public content and retargeted follow-up.

Treat allocations as forecasts, not facts.

### Gate 3 — Launch warm proof first

- Contact the highest-fit people personally.
- Ask prior attendees/referrers for a small, specific introduction or share.
- Tailor the first sentence; keep the body consistent and honest.
- Ask one low-friction next step.

### Gate 4 — Publish a demonstrated mechanism

Show how the experience works: goal → operating roles → knowledge base → playbooks → review cadence → measurable action. Avoid generic technology-revolution claims.

### Gate 5 — Follow up from evidence

Follow up with people who replied, asked, clicked, or were introduced. Address the actual objection. Do not chase silence indefinitely.

### Gate 6 — Reallocate daily

At least twice daily, compare registrations and qualified conversations by source. Shift effort to channels producing verified movement while respecting guardrails.

## Output contract

- verified fact sheet;
- source-tagged registration path or approved conversational CTA;
- segmented prospect/referrer/partner queues;
- direct, alumni, partner, and public-copy variants;
- live scorecard;
- daily continue/adapt/stop decisions.

## Metrics

- Outcome: confirmed registrations.
- Leading: qualified replies, conversations, partner shares, page visits, checkout starts.
- Inputs: qualified personal contacts, follow-ups, partner asks, approved posts.
- Guardrails: complaints, opt-outs, wrong facts, refunds, bad-fit registrations, delivery overload.

## Failure modes

- Sending traffic to a stale or broken page.
- Treating all contacts as equally qualified.
- Posting repeatedly without direct conversation.
- Using unverified scarcity, testimonials, or outcomes.
- Measuring reach while ignoring confirmed registrations.
- Changing message and channel simultaneously, destroying learning.

## Guardrails

- Never publish or send unverified event, price, outcome, scarcity, or testimonial claims.
- Require the business owner's approval before external outreach, publication, paid spend, or changing a live page.
- Stop a channel when complaints, opt-outs, wrong-fit demand, or delivery risk crosses its threshold.
- Keep private contact and purchase data out of campaign artifacts; store only aggregate evidence.
