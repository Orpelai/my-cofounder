# Problem
Teams often remember an event as a story shaped by hierarchy, outcome bias, and recency.
The U.S. Army uses the after-action review as a guided analysis of performance to improve
future execution, focusing on task standards, specific observations, and self-discovery.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Was the intended outcome and standard defined?
- Is observable evidence available?
- Can the people who performed the work participate?
- Is another iteration or reusable practice likely?
- Can the discussion occur soon enough for memory to remain useful?
- Will leaders protect a candid, non-punitive learning environment?

# Required inputs

- Original intent, plan, task standard, and success measures.
- Timeline and observable results.
- Perspectives from participants, customers, operators, and observers.
- Relevant artifacts, logs, metrics, and decisions.
- Known constraints and environmental changes.
- Previous lessons or standard work.

# Roles

- **Facilitator:** guides evidence-based self-discovery.
- **Event owner:** states intent and accepts improvement commitments.
- **Participants:** provide observations from their positions.
- **Recorder:** captures lessons, owners, and changed standards.
- **Action owners:** implement and verify follow-up.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Convene as soon as practical after the event.
2. Restate purpose: improve performance, not assign blame.
3. Review what was supposed to happen and the governing standard.
4. Reconstruct what actually happened from evidence and multiple perspectives.
5. Identify where actual performance matched or diverged from intent.

**Gate 1 — factual baseline:** do not explain causes until the group distinguishes
verified observation, participant interpretation, and unresolved disagreement.

6. Ask why key differences occurred, including environmental and system conditions.
7. Identify strengths to sustain, not only weaknesses to fix.
8. Select the few lessons that materially affect future performance.
9. Convert each lesson into immediate retraining, process change, experiment, or escalation.
10. Assign owner, due date, evidence, and review condition.

**Gate 2 — actionable learning:** a lesson is not complete unless it changes a decision,
standard, capability, or next iteration.

11. Share lessons with affected teams without exposing unnecessary personal detail.
12. Verify follow-up and test the revised practice.
13. Update standard work only after the change demonstrates value.

# Output contract

- Intended outcome and standard.
- Evidence-based actual timeline.
- Sustains, improves, and unresolved questions.
- Root or contributing factors with confidence.
- Follow-up actions, owners, due dates, and verification evidence.
- Updated practice or explicit reason no change is warranted.

# Metrics

- Time from event to review.
- Participation of relevant roles.
- Follow-up actions closed and verified.
- Recurrence of identified failure modes.
- Improvement in the next comparable iteration.
- Lessons incorporated into standard work.

# Failure modes

- Turning the review into a leader's lecture.
- Starting with blame or individual evaluation.
- Debating explanations before agreeing on events.
- Recording generic lessons such as “communicate better.”
- Ignoring what should be sustained.
- Closing actions without evidence of changed performance.
- Publishing sensitive operational details too broadly.

# Guardrails

- Keep the review candid, specific, and non-punitive.
- Separate learning review from misconduct or formal personnel processes.
- Use observable evidence and invite multiple perspectives.
- Protect safety, privacy, security, and legally privileged information.
- Preserve disagreement when evidence cannot resolve it.
- Do not rewrite the original plan or metrics after seeing the outcome.

# Adaptation notes
Army doctrine is designed for training and operations. This version translates the
four core questions into business work while preserving standards, observed performance,
facilitated self-discovery, and follow-up. Keep routine reviews short; expand serious
incidents into a separately governed investigation when needed.
