# Problem
Teams can contain talented people and still underperform because members do not speak
openly, depend on one another reliably, understand the work, find meaning, or see impact.
Google's Project Aristotle found that how a team works together mattered more than
many composition variables in the teams studied, with psychological safety most important.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Can people admit mistakes and ask basic questions without punishment or ridicule?
- Do teammates deliver what they promise?
- Are goals, roles, and decision processes clear?
- Does each person connect the work to personal meaning?
- Can the team explain its impact on customers or the organization?
- Is the problem team-wide, or does it require individual support or formal intervention?

# Required inputs

- Clearly bounded, genuinely interdependent team.
- Team purpose, current goals, roles, and recent outcome evidence.
- Anonymous or confidential pulse responses where feasible.
- Observable examples of helpful and harmful behaviors.
- Facilitator who can protect candor and avoid attribution.
- Leadership commitment to act on findings.

# Roles

- **Team lead:** models fallibility, curiosity, and accountability.
- **Facilitator:** collects input and runs a non-punitive discussion.
- **Team members:** interpret patterns and propose behavioral experiments.
- **People/HR partner:** supports sensitive or systemic issues.
- **Experiment owner:** tracks one agreed behavior and outcome.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define the team, its interdependence, and the outcomes it owns.
2. Explain that the exercise improves the system, not individual ratings.
3. Collect a short anonymous pulse on psychological safety, dependability,
   structure and clarity, meaning, and impact.
4. Combine survey patterns with delivery and stakeholder evidence.
5. Share only aggregated results and invite silent reflection first.

**Gate 1 — safety to discuss:** if retaliation, abuse, or severe power imbalance is
plausible, stop the group exercise and route through a protected formal process.

6. Ask for concrete situations, not judgments about personalities.
7. Identify one dynamic that most constrains team outcomes.
8. Co-design one or two observable behavior experiments for two to four weeks.
9. Define owner, frequency, expected signal, and guardrail.
10. Examples: dissent round before decisions, blameless error review, explicit role map,
    weekly commitment check, customer-impact opening, or leader “what am I missing?” prompt.

**Gate 2 — experiment quality:** proceed only when the behavior is observable,
within team control, psychologically safe, and tied to an outcome signal.

11. Run the experiment, notice unintended effects, and adjust in the moment.
12. Repeat the pulse and review outcome evidence.
13. Adopt, revise, or stop the behavior; record the lesson without identifying respondents.

# Output contract

- Team boundary and outcome definition.
- Aggregated five-dynamics baseline.
- Concrete behavior examples and current constraint.
- One-page experiment contract with owner and review date.
- Follow-up result, decision, and next improvement.

# Metrics

- Aggregated psychological-safety pulse.
- Commitment reliability and overdue handoffs.
- Goal, role, and decision-right clarity.
- Meeting participation distribution and dissent surfaced before closure.
- Error reporting latency and repeated avoidable failures.
- Team outcome and stakeholder measures relevant to its work.

# Failure modes

- Treating psychological safety as comfort or freedom from accountability.
- Publishing individual responses or hunting for who said what.
- Running a survey and taking no visible action.
- Blaming “culture” for unclear priorities or impossible workload.
- Copying Google's findings as universal causal law.
- Expecting vulnerable disclosure before leaders change their own behavior.

# Guardrails

- Psychological safety supports candor and learning; it does not excuse harmful behavior.
- Keep responses aggregated, confidential, and separate from performance reviews.
- Escalate harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or safety issues appropriately.
- Pair voice with clear standards, dependability, and consequences for misconduct.
- Present Project Aristotle as evidence from Google's context, not a universal guarantee.

# Adaptation notes

Google's five dynamics guide diagnosis. The pulse cadence, decision gates, and behavior
experiment contract are operating adaptations. For teams under five people, use careful
facilitation because anonymity may be impossible; do not promise what cannot be protected.
