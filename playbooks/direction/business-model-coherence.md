# Problem

An attractive offer can still fail when acquisition, delivery, monetization, capabilities, or costs contradict one another. A shared system model is needed before investment.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Is there one defined customer segment and payer?
- Does the value proposition explain an important outcome?
- Are channels and relationships affordable and reachable?
- Do revenue mechanics fit buying behavior?
- Can key activities, resources, and partners deliver at target margin?

Run the test when any answer is unknown or when two blocks conflict.

# Required inputs

- Segment-specific customer and purchasing evidence.
- Offer, price, usage, conversion, retention, and willingness-to-pay data.
- Acquisition and delivery workflow.
- Direct and indirect cost model.
- Capability, partner, channel, dependency, and regulatory constraints.
- Current model plus at least one alternative.

# Roles

- **Model owner:** founder or business-model lead.
- **Customer owner:** validates segments, jobs, channels, and relationships.
- **Delivery owner:** validates activities, resources, quality, and capacity.
- **Economics owner:** validates revenue, costs, cash, and sensitivity.
- **Decider/challenger:** approves tests and exposes contradictions.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define the unit of analysis: one offer for one coherent segment and buying context.
2. Fill the nine Business Model Canvas blocks with current evidence.
3. Mark every statement as fact, inference, or hypothesis.
4. **Gate 1 — segment coherence:** separate users, buyers, and materially different segments.
5. Trace the customer path: awareness → evaluation → purchase → delivery → retention/expansion.
6. Test fit among value proposition, channels, relationships, and revenue streams.
7. Trace the operating path: key resources → activities → partners → delivered outcome.
8. Test whether cost structure follows the operating path at expected volume.
9. Calculate contribution margin, CAC payback, cash timing, retention sensitivity, and capacity.
10. **Gate 2 — economic viability:** stop or redesign if realistic volume worsens cash or margin beyond limits.
11. Identify cross-block contradictions and the assumptions most capable of breaking the model.
12. Generate at least two coherent model variants, changing linked blocks together.
13. Rank assumptions by impact, uncertainty, and test cost.
14. **Gate 3 — evidence plan:** require behavioral tests for the top viability assumptions.
15. Run the smallest tests: paid pilot, channel test, delivery simulation, partner quote, or pricing commitment.
16. Update the model from evidence; do not rewrite prior observations.
17. Decide to advance, pivot, pause, or stop and set the next review trigger.

# Output contract

- Dated Business Model Canvas for one unit of analysis.
- Cross-block coherence and contradiction register.
- Unit-economics model with base/downside/upside cases.
- Ranked assumption ledger and test backlog.
- Selected model, rejected variants, and rationale.
- Next investment gate, owner, evidence threshold, and deadline.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Contribution margin, cash conversion, retention, sustainable revenue, and promised customer outcome.

## Leading

- Paid or costly customer commitments.
- Channel conversion/acquisition cost and delivery time/utilization/variable cost.

## Guardrail

- Runway, refund rate, support burden, concentration, compliance, and service quality.
- Unfunded working-capital requirement.

# Failure modes

- Treating the canvas as a one-time poster.
- Mixing segments with different jobs, buyers, or economics.
- Filling blocks with nouns instead of testable mechanisms.
- Ignoring cash timing and only modeling accounting revenue.
- Optimizing one block while breaking another.
- Calling a coherent narrative a validated model.

# Guardrails

- Every critical claim needs a source, confidence, and owner.
- Recalculate economics when price, channel, segment, or delivery changes.
- Preserve downside assumptions and capacity constraints.
- Do not scale before demand and delivery evidence meet the investment gate.
- Never hide externalities, legal obligations, or customer harm outside the canvas.

# Adaptation notes

The Business Model Canvas is an originator/vendor modeling framework. It makes assumptions visible; it does not validate them. Viability must be demonstrated through behavior, economics, and reliable delivery.

For a solo business, maintain one canvas per materially different offer/segment and link each block to a source or experiment.
