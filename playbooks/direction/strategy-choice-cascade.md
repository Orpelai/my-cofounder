# Problem

A list of ambitions is not a strategy. The business needs a coherent theory of where it will compete, how it will win there, and what must be true operationally.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Can leaders state one winning aspiration without listing projects?
- Are target customers, offers, channels, and geographies explicitly bounded?
- Is “how to win” specific to each chosen arena?
- Would competitors be unable or unwilling to copy the critical capabilities?
- Do management systems reinforce the choices?

Use this playbook when two or more answers are “no” or unsupported.

# Required inputs

- Current goal, baseline, deadline, and non-goals.
- Customer, market, competitor, and unit-economics evidence.
- Current capabilities, constraints, and management systems.
- Two to four plausible strategic alternatives.
- Named assumptions and evidence confidence.

# Roles

- **Decider:** founder/CEO.
- **Facilitator:** strategy owner; protects choice quality and evidence.
- **Contributors:** customer, product, commercial, finance, and operations owners.
- **Challenger:** tests copyability, feasibility, and hidden trade-offs.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. **Frame the choice.** Define the decision horizon and measurable meaning of winning.
2. Draft the **winning aspiration** as a customer-and-business outcome.
3. **Gate 1 — meaningful win:** reject aspirations that are generic, activity-based, or unmeasurable.
4. Generate at least two matched **where-to-play/how-to-win** pairs.
5. Bound where-to-play by customer, need, offer, channel, geography, and value-chain position.
6. State how-to-win as a defensible advantage mechanism, not “be best” or “innovate.”
7. **Gate 2 — matched heart:** reject any arena without a credible advantage in that arena.
8. Identify the few **must-have capabilities** required by each candidate.
9. Run the “can’t/won’t” test: why can competitors not copy, or why would copying harm them?
10. **Gate 3 — durable capability:** downgrade candidates whose capabilities are generic or easily matched.
11. Specify **enabling management systems**: metrics, decisions, incentives, routines, talent, and capital.
12. **Gate 4 — operability:** reject candidates the business cannot build, fund, or govern in time.
13. Toggle back through all five choices until they reinforce one another.
14. Compare alternatives against evidence, downside, reversibility, and expected value.
15. Decide, name exclusions, assign capability bets, and set review conditions.

# Output contract

- One-page cascade containing all five choices.
- Explicit where-not-to-play and how-not-to-win choices.
- Capability gap and management-system change list.
- Assumption ledger with evidence, confidence, owner, and test.
- 90-day action sequence and strategic review date.
- Decision record explaining why alternatives were rejected.

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Growth or value-delivery metric in the chosen arena.
- Advantage metric tied to the how-to-win mechanism.

## Leading

- Share of priority resources aligned to the cascade.
- Completion and validation of critical capability milestones.
- Strategic assumptions tested per review cycle.

## Guardrail

- Cash runway, concentration risk, customer harm, and core-service performance.
- Number of unplanned exceptions to where-to-play exclusions.

# Failure modes

- Treating the five boxes as a linear waterfall instead of an iterative system.
- Locking where-to-play before pairing it with how-to-win.
- Spending heavily on aspiration while neglecting capabilities and systems.
- Calling standard industry capabilities “distinctive.”
- Choosing a compromise portfolio that avoids real exclusions.
- Confusing confidence, narrative quality, or executive agreement with evidence.

# Guardrails

- Keep at least two real alternatives alive until the operability gate.
- Label facts, assumptions, and recommendations separately.
- Do not approve a strategy without a measurable win and explicit exclusions.
- Do not make irreversible commitments before testing the riskiest assumptions.
- Reopen the cascade when external conditions or core evidence materially change.

# Adaptation notes

This is an originator-authored practitioner framework. Use it as a disciplined operating hypothesis, then validate the chosen strategy with customer behavior, economics, and competitive response.

For a solo business, one person may hold several roles, but should still separate decider, evidence collector, and challenger passes. Compress the working session; do not remove the decision gates.
