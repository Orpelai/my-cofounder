# Problem

Demand activity targets only people likely to buy now, producing a narrow audience,
rising acquisition costs, and weak future preference. The business captures demand
but does not create the memory structures that make it an easy future choice.

# Trigger diagnostic

- What percentage of category buyers purchase in a typical year?
- Is the replacement cycle measured, estimated, or merely assumed?
- Does paid activity repeatedly reach the same small audience?
- Among qualified category buyers, how many recall the brand unaided?
- Which situations cause buyers to enter the category?
- Does pipeline fall immediately when activation spend is reduced?

**Gate 1 — category validity:** Continue only when the category, buyer population,
and approximate purchase cycle can be stated with evidence.

# Required inputs

- Defined category and addressable buyer population
- Purchase-frequency or replacement-cycle evidence
- Priority segments and buying situations
- Current reach, frequency, awareness, search, and pipeline data
- Distinctive brand assets and approved positioning
- Channel costs, budget limits, and sales-capacity constraints

# Roles

- **Decider:** Founder or growth leader
- **Owner:** Demand lead
- **Contributors:** Brand, content, performance marketing, sales, analytics
- **Reviewer:** Finance or operator responsible for unit economics

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Estimate annual category entry from customer data, CRM history, research, and
   explicit assumptions. Report a range, not false precision.
2. Separate the market into current buyers and future buyers for planning purposes.
3. Identify 3–7 category entry points: situations, needs, or triggers that bring
   buyers into the market.
4. Rank entry points by frequency, commercial value, competitive ownership, and
   fit with the offer.
5. Audit mental availability: unaided recall, branded search, direct traffic,
   recognition of distinctive assets, and inclusion in consideration sets.
6. Define future-demand communication around memorable category entry points,
   consistent brand codes, broad qualified reach, and repeated exposure.
7. Define active-demand capture separately: search, retargeting, comparison,
   conversion content, sales response, and qualification.

**Gate 2 — portfolio choice:** Approve separate objectives, audiences, creative,
channels, and time horizons for future-demand building and current-demand capture.

8. Set frequency and reach controls to avoid overserving a tiny audience.
9. Run a minimum viable memory-building flight long enough for meaningful exposure.
10. Maintain activation coverage so buyers entering the market can act immediately.
11. Review leading memory signals monthly and commercial outcomes over the buying
    cycle; do not judge long-term activity solely by last-click leads.
12. Reallocate only when evidence shows saturation, weak memory encoding, poor
    category fit, or activation capacity constraints.

**Gate 3 — continue, revise, or stop:** Continue when qualified reach and memory
signals improve without guardrail breach. Revise creative or entry points when reach
exists but memory does not. Stop when the category thesis or economics fail.

# Output contract

- Category-demand estimate with range, method, and confidence
- Current-buyer/future-buyer planning model
- Ranked category-entry-point map
- Mental-availability baseline
- Two-lane demand portfolio with budget, channels, creative, and cadence
- Measurement plan with review dates and named data owners

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Qualified pipeline and revenue over the relevant buying cycle
- Share of market or category penetration where measurable
- Brand inclusion in actual consideration sets

## Leading

- Qualified reach and effective frequency
- Unaided and aided brand recall
- Association with priority category entry points
- Branded search, direct traffic, and target-account engagement

## Guardrail

- CAC and payback period
- Frequency concentration and audience saturation
- Lead quality and sales capacity
- Retention, refunds, and contribution margin

# Failure modes

- Treating “95–5” as a universal measured split
- Calling untargeted mass reach “brand building”
- Using the same creative and KPI for both demand lanes
- Optimizing future-demand activity to last-click attribution
- Building awareness without distinctive assets or category cues
- Ignoring current buyers while waiting for long-term effects

# Guardrails

- State the observed purchase cycle and uncertainty.
- Do not claim advertising creates category demand on its own.
- Use category-qualified reach, not raw impressions, as the denominator.
- Preserve an activation path for buyers ready to act.
- Require financial and operational capacity before scaling reach.

# Adaptation notes

The 95–5 split is a memorable illustration based on infrequent B2B purchasing, not a
constant. Recalculate the in-market share from the category’s replacement cycle.
For young categories, add education and problem recognition; for mature categories,
emphasize distinctive memory and category-entry-point ownership.
