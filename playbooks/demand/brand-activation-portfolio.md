# Problem

The marketing system overinvests in either immediate response or broad awareness.
Activation alone harvests existing demand and can become progressively expensive;
brand activity without commercial discipline may create attention that never
converts. A portfolio is needed because the two jobs operate on different horizons.

# Trigger diagnostic

- What share of spend and staff time serves brand versus activation?
- Are the two jobs defined by objective, or merely by channel?
- Is lead efficiency declining as frequency rises?
- Does activation perform better among people already familiar with the brand?
- Are brand results evaluated before enough time or reach has accumulated?
- Can finance tolerate the proposed learning horizon?

**Gate 1 — diagnose the imbalance:** Continue only when current allocation, funnel
economics, time horizon, and a plausible portfolio problem are documented.

# Required inputs

- Revenue target, buying cycle, and cash constraints
- Historic spend, reach, leads, pipeline, revenue, and margin
- Audience definitions and market size
- Brand-awareness and consideration baseline
- Channel-level incrementality or attribution evidence
- Creative assets, distinctive brand codes, and sales capacity

# Roles

- **Decider:** Founder or CMO
- **Owner:** Marketing lead
- **Contributors:** Brand, demand generation, sales, analytics, creative
- **Reviewer:** Finance

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Classify every major activity by primary job:
   - **Brand:** build salience and emotional memory across future buyers.
   - **Activation:** prompt and convert buyers with near-term intent.
2. Flag mixed campaigns whose audience, creative, or KPI prevents either job from
   being performed well.
3. Establish separate baselines for reach and memory, then response and conversion.
4. Review cash horizon, category maturity, buying cycle, and current brand strength.
5. Use the published 46% brand / 54% activation finding only as a discussion
   benchmark, never as an automatic allocation.
6. Create at least three allocation scenarios with expected upside, timing,
   uncertainty, and downside.

**Gate 2 — approve portfolio:** Select a time-bound allocation with explicit reasons,
minimum activation coverage, and a financial stop condition.

7. Give brand activity broad qualified reach, consistent assets, emotional
   memorability, and category relevance.
8. Give activation activity tighter intent signals, rational proof, a clear offer,
   fast response, and measurable conversion.
9. Set distinct evaluation windows: short for activation, long enough to observe
   memory and commercial carryover for brand.
10. Track interaction effects, including whether brand-exposed audiences convert
    more efficiently, while avoiding causal claims from correlation alone.
11. Review activation weekly, portfolio health monthly, and long-term commercial
    effects quarterly or according to the buying cycle.
12. Rebalance when marginal returns, cash needs, market conditions, or evidence
    materially change.

**Gate 3 — rebalance:** Shift budget only with documented marginal evidence,
guardrail status, and the expected consequence for both time horizons.

# Output contract

- Activity classification and current allocation
- Three budget scenarios with assumptions
- Approved brand/activation portfolio
- Separate creative and channel briefs
- Dual-horizon scorecard
- Rebalancing rules and review calendar

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Revenue and gross profit over short and long horizons
- Market share or penetration where measurable
- Incremental pipeline and customer acquisition

## Leading

- Brand: qualified reach, salience, recall, consideration, distinctive-asset recall
- Activation: response, qualified leads, opportunity conversion, sales velocity
- Portfolio: activation efficiency among brand-aware versus unaware cohorts

## Guardrail

- CAC, payback, burn, and contribution margin
- Lead quality and sales acceptance
- Audience saturation and creative wear-out
- Retention and refund rate

# Failure modes

- Treating channels as inherently “brand” or “activation”
- Applying 46/54 regardless of stage, cash, or category
- Measuring brand with immediate lead targets
- Measuring activation only with platform-attributed conversions
- Using rational product detail as long-term memory creative by default
- Cutting brand after a short window or activation after one weak week

# Guardrails

- Preserve enough activation to capture available demand.
- Do not fund long-horizon work beyond the company’s cash tolerance.
- Require category linkage and distinctive branding in brand creative.
- Use experiments or triangulation before claiming incrementality.
- Record allocation changes so performance remains comparable.

# Adaptation notes

The published allocation is a portfolio benchmark derived from effectiveness cases.
An unknown startup may require more activation and category education; an established
brand in a mature category may justify more memory building. Define each lane by its
job and horizon, then calibrate the allocation from evidence.
