# Problem

Partnership enthusiasm precedes strategic fit, due diligence, operating design, or
governance. The agreement launches without clear incentives and then stalls, creates
dependency, or destroys trust when assumptions diverge.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Why partner instead of build or buy?
- What value can only or best be created jointly?
- Are incentives reciprocal, measurable, and durable?
- Who owns the alliance on each side?
- Which operational, cultural, legal, data, and concentration risks exist?
- Can either party exit without unacceptable customer harm?

**Gate 1 — alliance thesis:** Continue only when partnership has a credible advantage
over build, buy, transact, or do nothing.

# Required inputs

- Company strategy and alliance-specific objective
- Build/buy/partner comparison
- Partner landscape and selection criteria
- Customer value and joint economic model
- Capability, cultural, financial, security, and legal diligence
- Proposed operating model, governance, metrics, and exit conditions

# Roles

- **Executive sponsors:** One per organization
- **Alliance manager:** Accountable lifecycle owner
- **Deciders:** Authorized leaders for investment and contract
- **Contributors:** Product, sales, operations, finance, legal, security, customer success
- **Governance body:** Joint steering group with defined decision rights

# Procedure with decision gates

1. **Alliance-specific strategy:** Connect the alliance to business strategy and
   define the capability, market, customer, or innovation advantage sought.
2. Compare build, buy, partner, and no-action options on value, speed, control,
   investment, reversibility, and risk.

**Gate 2 — partner route:** Approve partnering only when the strategic thesis and
shared-value hypothesis beat realistic alternatives.

3. **Analysis and selection:** Define candidate criteria and diligence strategic,
   operational, financial, cultural, reputational, data, and security fit.
4. **Trust and value-creating negotiation:** Jointly map value, contributions,
   incentives, constraints, intellectual property, data rights, risk, and obligations.
5. Confirm that negotiated commitments can be executed by actual teams.

**Gate 3 — negotiate or stop:** Proceed only when both sides have meaningful value,
aligned incentives, credible sponsors, and resolvable diligence findings.

6. **Operational planning:** Treat the joint plan as a test of prior assumptions.
   Define customer journey, workstreams, capacity, enablement, dependencies,
   economics, service levels, metrics, and escalation.
7. **Structuring and governance:** Finalize legal and organizational structures,
   decision rights, risk/reward formula, funding, reporting, dispute handling,
   change control, and exit terms.

**Gate 4 — launch readiness:** Require named owners, funded work, trained teams,
approved controls, measurable milestones, and a rollback or containment plan.

8. **Launching and managing:** Hold a joint kickoff, confirm understandings, execute
   the launch plan, operate governance, resolve conflicts, and measure joint value.
9. Review performance and assumptions at a fixed cadence; distinguish execution
   failure from a broken alliance thesis.
10. **Transform, innovate, or exit gracefully:** Renew, expand, redesign, or close
    based on evidence and future fit.

**Gate 5 — lifecycle decision:** Continue only when mutual value exceeds cost and
risk. Otherwise transform or exit according to agreed customer and data protections.

# Output contract

- Build/buy/partner decision record
- Partner thesis and selection scorecard
- Due-diligence findings and risk treatments
- Joint value proposition and economic model
- Operational plan and responsibility matrix
- Governance charter, scorecard, escalation, renewal, and exit terms

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Alliance-sourced or influenced revenue and gross profit
- Customer value delivered
- Strategic capability or market access achieved
- Renewal, expansion, or successful exit

## Leading

- Joint pipeline and activation milestones
- Enablement completion and partner participation
- Delivery milestones and issue-resolution time
- Sponsor and governance attendance

## Guardrail

- Concentration and dependency exposure
- Customer complaints and service failures
- Data, security, legal, and compliance incidents
- Margin dilution, channel conflict, and brand risk

# Failure modes

- Selecting a famous partner without strategic fit
- Signing before operational owners validate commitments
- One-sided economics or ambiguous contribution
- Governance with meetings but no decision rights
- Counting pipeline with no attribution rules
- Staying because of sunk cost or exiting without customer protection

# Guardrails

- Partnership authority, data access, and commitments must be explicit.
- Do not share customer or confidential data without lawful authorization.
- Preserve independent measurement and audit rights.
- Cap concentration risk and define continuity alternatives.
- Include executable change, dispute, termination, and transition provisions.

# Adaptation notes

Use all seven phases for strategic, interdependent relationships. Compress the method
for smaller channel or co-marketing agreements, but retain thesis, diligence, owner,
metrics, governance, and exit. Regulated or data-intensive alliances require deeper
legal, privacy, security, and resilience review.
