# Problem

Sellers present capabilities before the customer has clarified the problem, its
consequences, and the value of solving it. The buyer receives information but does
not develop urgency, ownership, or a defensible reason to advance.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Did the last call spend more time demonstrating than investigating?
- Can the buyer state the problem in their own words?
- Are consequences specific enough to justify change?
- Is value tied to outcomes the buyer cares about?
- Did the call end with an explicit advance rather than a vague continuation?

**Gate 1 — call fit:** Use SPIN when meaningful discovery remains and the buyer has
permission, time, and enough context for a diagnostic conversation.

# Required inputs

- Account and stakeholder context
- Known business priorities and hypotheses
- A small set of purposeful SPIN questions
- Relevant proof and capabilities, held until needs are established
- Desired advance and acceptable fallback
- CRM record of prior statements and commitments

# Roles

- **Owner:** Account executive or founder-seller
- **Customer participants:** Problem owner, affected users, relevant decision makers
- **Observer or coach:** Optional sales manager
- **Recorder:** Seller, with consent and applicable privacy controls

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Prepare hypotheses, not conclusions. Research facts that should not waste call
   time as Situation questions.
2. Open with purpose, relevance, time check, and permission to explore.
3. Ask limited **Situation** questions to fill only material context gaps.
4. Ask **Problem** questions that surface difficulties, dissatisfaction, friction,
   risk, or missed outcomes.
5. Reflect the buyer’s language and test understanding.

**Gate 2 — problem ownership:** Do not advance to implications until the buyer
confirms a material problem. If none exists, disqualify or return to learning.

6. Ask **Implication** questions about operational, financial, strategic, personal,
   and downstream consequences.
7. Quantify only with buyer-supported data; label estimates and ranges.
8. Ask **Need-payoff** questions that let the buyer articulate the benefit of solving
   the problem and the value of a better future state.
9. Summarize situation, problem, implication, and desired payoff; ask for correction.

**Gate 3 — capability demonstration:** Demonstrate only capabilities directly tied
to needs the buyer acknowledged. Defer irrelevant features.

10. Connect evidence and proof to each confirmed need.
11. Surface remaining uncertainty, stakeholders, and decision requirements.
12. Seek an explicit advance: agreed next meeting, data exchange, technical
    validation, stakeholder introduction, or commercial step.

**Gate 4 — commitment:** Record an advance only when owner, action, and date are
mutually clear. A polite “keep in touch” is not progress.

# Output contract

- Confirmed problem statement in customer language
- Implication map with evidence and quantified ranges
- Buyer-stated need-payoff outcomes
- Capability-to-need mapping
- Open questions and disqualification signals
- Mutual next step with owner and date

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Discovery-to-qualified-opportunity conversion
- Win rate and gross margin
- Reduction in no-decision losses

## Leading

- Ratio of buyer talk to seller talk
- Confirmed problems with evidence
- Buyer-articulated implications and payoffs
- Calls ending in a concrete advance

## Guardrail

- Customer-reported call quality
- Discounting and complaint rates
- Sales-cycle length by segment
- Privacy and recording compliance

# Failure modes

- Turning SPIN into a rigid interrogation sequence
- Asking basic Situation questions that research could answer
- Leading the buyer toward a predetermined pain
- Exaggerating implications or manufacturing urgency
- Pitching after the first problem statement
- Treating a calendar booking as proof of qualification

# Guardrails

- Ask permission and preserve the buyer’s ability to stop.
- Use open, neutral questions; never fabricate consequences.
- Distinguish buyer facts, seller hypotheses, and estimates.
- Do not diagnose beyond professional competence.
- Demonstrate only after confirmed need and summarize accurately.

# Adaptation notes

SPIN is a logical framework, not a mandatory script or fixed sequence. Short-cycle
sales need fewer Situation and Implication questions. Complex sales may require
multiple conversations with different stakeholders before the complete need picture
is reliable.
