# Problem

The vendor wins a functional evaluation but loses the organizational decision.
Members of the buying group must protect budgets, careers, compliance, operations,
and internal relationships. A decision that cannot be defended may stall even when
the product appears superior.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Who recommends, uses, approves, blocks, pays, procures, and bears implementation risk?
- Which members know and trust the vendor before active evaluation?
- Can each stakeholder explain why the decision remains defensible if results vary?
- What evidence addresses personal, functional, and collective risk?
- Is the champion carrying the internal case alone?
- Does a stall reflect disagreement, risk, missing proof, or weak product fit?

**Gate 1 — diagnose confidence versus capability:** Continue only after confirming
that requirements are credible and the principal gap is group confidence or consensus.

# Required inputs

- Buying-group map with influence, role, concerns, and access route
- Functional requirements and known product gaps
- Decision criteria and process
- Segment-matched customer evidence
- Security, legal, financial, operational, and implementation proof
- Champion’s internal narrative and unresolved objections

# Roles

- **Owner:** Account executive
- **Partner:** Champion or internal recommender
- **Contributors:** Marketing, customer advocacy, solutions, security, legal, CSM
- **Reviewer:** Sales manager
- **Customer decider:** Economic or authorized decision owner

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Map the buying group, including less visible finance, legal, procurement,
   security, operations, and executive stakeholders.
2. Record each member’s desired outcome, feared downside, evidence threshold,
   familiarity, influence, and current stance.
3. Separate rational capability gaps from confidence gaps.
4. Identify the defensibility question: “If this choice underperforms, what evidence
   shows the decision was still reasonable?”
5. Build a proof matrix using relevant peers, comparable use cases, implementation
   plans, reference customers, controls, and realistic limitations.

**Gate 2 — proof sufficiency:** Advance only when important claims have verifiable,
segment-relevant proof and known product limitations are disclosed.

6. Help the champion build an internal decision case: problem, alternatives, value,
   risks, mitigations, implementation, economics, and decision request.
7. Create role-specific materials without changing the underlying truth:
   - Finance: economics, sensitivity, and downside.
   - Legal/security: controls, obligations, and exceptions.
   - Users/operations: workflow, adoption, support, and transition.
   - Executives: strategic value, accountability, and reversibility.
8. Seek ethical multithreading through the champion or agreed process.
9. Add peer advocacy where permission exists: references, reviews, communities,
   customer evidence, or direct conversations.
10. Run a buyer-side premortem and answer risks with evidence, owners, and fallback.

**Gate 3 — consensus readiness:** Proceed to commercial closure only when unresolved
objections, authority, implementation ownership, and decision path are explicit.

11. Monitor stakeholder changes and refresh the map.
12. On win or loss, record which confidence signals actually influenced the decision.

# Output contract

- Buying-group influence and confidence map
- Stakeholder-specific risk and proof matrix
- Defensible internal decision brief
- Ethical multithreading plan
- Peer-advocacy assets and permissions
- Consensus gaps, owners, dates, and go/no-go recommendation

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Buying-group win rate
- No-decision and stall rate
- Time from shortlist to decision

## Leading

- Coverage of high-influence stakeholders
- Brand familiarity across the group
- Verified objections resolved
- Relevant peer-proof engagements
- Completion of internal business case and implementation plan

## Guardrail

- Product-fit and implementation-risk exceptions
- Customer trust or complaint signals
- Contact-consent compliance
- Discounting and promise accuracy

# Failure modes

- Confusing broad awareness with stakeholder confidence
- Hiding product gaps behind social proof
- Going around the champion without consent
- Treating reported multipliers as guaranteed effects
- Producing inconsistent stories for different functions
- Overloading buyers with undifferentiated case studies

# Guardrails

- Product capability remains a prerequisite.
- Use only permissioned, truthful customer evidence.
- Disclose relevant limitations and implementation risk.
- Never manufacture consensus, urgency, or peer recommendations.
- Keep one coherent decision case across stakeholder variants.
