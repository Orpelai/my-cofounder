# Problem

Complex opportunities appear healthy while critical evidence is missing: quantified
value, authority, evaluation rules, approval steps, procurement, consequential pain,
an effective champion, or the real competition. Optimistic CRM stages hide risk.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Which MEDDPICC elements have buyer-verifiable evidence?
- Is forecast status based on facts or seller confidence?
- Can the team explain how the customer reaches and documents a decision?
- Does the champion have power, influence, credibility, and a reason to act?
- Are “no decision,” internal build, and competing initiatives tracked as competition?

**Gate 1 — framework fit:** Apply fully only when deal complexity, value, and risk
justify structured inspection. For smaller deals, use the few decisive elements.

# Required inputs

- Opportunity value, stage, history, stakeholders, and next steps
- Customer-confirmed problem and implications
- Business-case assumptions and source data
- Technical, economic, and relationship decision criteria
- Decision, procurement, legal, security, and contracting processes
- Competitive alternatives, including status quo

# Roles

- **Owner:** Account executive
- **Coach:** Sales manager
- **Contributors:** Solutions, customer success, legal, finance, security
- **Customer roles:** Champion, Economic Buyer, evaluators, procurement stakeholders
- **Decider:** Forecast owner for internal commit category

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Create an evidence record for each element:
   - **Metrics:** quantified value of the solution.
   - **Economic Buyer:** person with overall buying authority.
   - **Decision Criteria:** technical, economic, and relationship factors.
   - **Decision Process:** how the buyer evaluates and approves.
   - **Paper Process:** procurement, legal, and contracting path.
   - **Implicate the Pain:** problem and broader consequences.
   - **Champion:** influential, credible person willing and able to help.
   - **Competition:** vendors, people, initiatives, internal build, or no action.
2. Grade each item `verified`, `supported`, `assumed`, or `unknown`, with source and
   verification date.
3. Quantify Metrics jointly where possible; include baseline, target, time horizon,
   owner, and calculation assumptions.
4. Map authority and distinguish a coach or friendly user from a Champion.

**Gate 2 — qualified pursuit:** Continue investing only when material pain, plausible
value, customer action, and a path to authority exist.

5. Reconstruct Decision Criteria and Process from customer evidence, not a generic
   procurement template.
6. Map the Paper Process backward from the required signature date.
7. Test the Champion with observable actions: access, information, guidance, and
   internal advocacy—not verbal enthusiasm.
8. Identify the strongest competing use of budget and the consequences of no action.
9. Convert every evidence gap into a dated action owned by seller or customer.
10. Review deal risk with the manager; challenge assumptions and duplicated evidence.

**Gate 3 — forecast category:** Commit only when the organization’s defined evidence
threshold is met. Otherwise classify honestly as upside, pipeline, or disqualified.

11. Reinspect after stakeholder, date, scope, price, or process changes.
12. Close the record with win/loss/no-decision evidence and calibration learning.

# Output contract

- MEDDPICC evidence map with status, source, owner, and freshness
- Quantified value case and assumption register
- Stakeholder and authority map
- Decision and paper-process timeline
- Competition map
- Gap-closing action plan and evidence-based forecast category

# Metrics

## Outcome

- Win rate and gross margin by deal class
- Forecast accuracy
- No-decision rate and sales-cycle duration

## Leading

- Percentage of elements with verified evidence
- Economic Buyer access
- Champion tests completed
- Decision/paper milestones completed on time
- Age of unresolved critical gaps

## Guardrail

- Seller time per opportunity
- Customer experience and complaint signals
- Discount rate
- Late-stage disqualification rate

# Failure modes

- Checkbox completion without evidence
- Backfilling fields to justify an optimistic forecast
- Calling any supportive contact a Champion
- Ignoring no decision as the primary competitor
- Treating customer dates as commitments without process validation
- Applying full enterprise overhead to small transactional deals

# Guardrails

- Never invent buyer evidence or authority.
- Record uncertainty explicitly and preserve contrary signals.
- Use the framework for inspection and execution, not coercion.
- Do not expose confidential customer information beyond authorized scope.
- Disqualify when evidence says expected value no longer justifies pursuit.

# Adaptation notes

Define organization-specific evidence thresholds by segment and deal size. Do not
create a universal numeric “MEDDPICC score” unless historical outcomes validate it;
a single fatal gap can matter more than a high aggregate score.
