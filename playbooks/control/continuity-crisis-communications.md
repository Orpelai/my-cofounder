# Problem

During disruption, teams may protect visible activity instead of essential functions,
while uncoordinated messages amplify harm. FEMA continuity guidance emphasizes sustaining
essential functions. The UK Government Communication Service guide provides a practical
structure for crisis communication planning.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Which functions must continue, at what minimum level, and for how long?
- Who has authority if normal leaders or systems are unavailable?
- What dependencies have no viable workaround?
- Which audiences need what information, through which trusted channels?
- Is there one verified operational picture and a cadence for updates?
- What thresholds activate, escalate, relocate, or recover the plan?

# Required inputs

- Essential functions and acceptable interruption periods.
- People, facilities, vendors, data, technology, and utility dependencies.
- Delegations of authority and orders of succession.
- Contact lists, alternate channels, and accessibility needs.
- Stakeholder and media map.
- Known facts, unknowns, risks, legal duties, and approved spokespersons.
- Recovery priorities and continuity exercise evidence.

# Roles

- **Crisis lead:** owns activation, priorities, and command rhythm.
- **Continuity lead:** sustains essential functions and workarounds.
- **Operations leads:** report verified status and execute recovery.
- **Communications lead:** owns audience strategy, messages, and channels.
- **Spokesperson:** communicates externally within approved facts.
- **Legal/privacy/safety reviewers:** assess consequential statements and actions.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Detect the disruption and verify the initial signal.
2. Assess impact on people, essential functions, dependencies, and obligations.
3. Establish an incident record, command roles, and a single operational picture.
4. Classify severity and set the next decision time.

**Gate 1 — activation:** activate continuity when an essential function is threatened
beyond tolerance or normal operations cannot coordinate an adequate response.

5. Protect life, safety, data, and critical assets.
6. Prioritize essential functions and activate alternate people, sites, systems, or vendors.
7. State confirmed facts, known unknowns, actions underway, and next update time.
8. Segment messages for employees, customers, partners, authorities, and media.
9. Use approved channels with backups; monitor questions and harmful misinformation.
10. Record decisions, message versions, delivery evidence, and operational changes.

**Gate 2 — escalation:** escalate when impact crosses authority, geographic, legal,
financial, or reputational thresholds, or when information integrity is lost.

11. Reassess essential-function performance and stakeholder needs on a fixed cadence.
12. Transition to recovery only when controls are stable and owners accept handoff.
13. Conduct an after-action review and update plans, contacts, exercises, and controls.

# Output contract

- Activation record, severity, incident lead, and next decision time.
- Essential-function status and workaround plan.
- Stakeholder-message matrix and version-controlled approved messages.
- Decision, action, dependency, and issue log.
- Recovery criteria, handoff, and after-action commitments.

# Metrics

- Time to detect, activate, and establish command.
- Essential-function downtime and service level.
- Time to first verified stakeholder update.
- Message approval and delivery latency.
- Reach, comprehension, correction, and inquiry-resolution rates.
- Recovery time and recurrence of known failure modes.

# Failure modes

- Speculating to fill an information vacuum.
- Sending identical messages to audiences with different needs.
- Depending on one leader, facility, vendor, or channel.
- Publishing operational detail that creates security or privacy risk.
- Declaring recovery before essential controls are stable.
- Failing to capture decisions for later learning.

# Guardrails

- Human safety and competent public authority take precedence.
- Communicate what is known, unknown, being done, and when more will follow.
- Never fabricate certainty, causes, casualties, timelines, or commitments.
- Protect personal, security-sensitive, legally privileged, and investigative information.
- Ensure accessibility, language, and offline alternatives for critical audiences.

# Adaptation notes
FEMA guidance is designed for continuity programs and government functions; the UK
guide focuses on communications. This playbook integrates their operating principles
for business use. Validate sector-specific duties and rehearse at least one channel failure.
