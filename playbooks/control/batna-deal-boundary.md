# Problem

Negotiators often compare a concrete offer with a vague hope, overvalue headline price,
or remain in a bad deal because time and identity have already been invested.
BATNA means the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: the action that will actually
be taken if the current negotiation ends without agreement.

# Trigger diagnostic

Use this playbook when at least two answers are unclear:

- What will we do if no agreement is reached?
- Is that alternative executable within the relevant time window?
- Have all material terms been normalized for a like-for-like comparison?
- What is the minimum complete package that beats the alternative?
- What evidence could strengthen or weaken our alternative before the deadline?

# Required inputs

- Decision deadline and negotiation scope.
- Current offer, including non-price terms.
- Realistic alternatives with owners, costs, timing, and probability.
- Objectives ranked as must-have, tradable, and optional.
- Legal, operational, reputational, and relationship constraints.
- Counterparty alternatives and incentives, marked as evidence or inference.

# Roles

- **Negotiation owner:** runs the process and maintains the comparison.
- **Decision owner:** accepts, rejects, or authorizes a counteroffer.
- **Domain reviewers:** validate legal, financial, technical, and delivery terms.
- **Red-team reviewer:** challenges optimistic assumptions and hidden switching costs.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Define the decision and the exact point at which no deal becomes the outcome.
2. Generate multiple alternatives without judging them prematurely.
3. Convert each alternative into an executable plan with next action, owner, and date.
4. Estimate the complete value of each alternative, including risk and switching cost.
5. Select the strongest credible alternative as the provisional BATNA.
6. Improve it where possible before relying on it.
7. Translate the current offer and BATNA into comparable dimensions.

**Gate 1 — BATNA credibility:** proceed only if the alternative is lawful, feasible,
funded, and available within the required time. Otherwise build a better alternative.

8. Set the reservation boundary: the worst complete deal still preferable to the BATNA.
9. Separate the internal boundary from the opening position and target package.
10. Identify trades that create value without crossing the reservation boundary.
11. Reassess the BATNA whenever timing, information, or counterpart behavior changes.

**Gate 2 — deal decision:** accept only when the complete verified package beats the
current BATNA and satisfies all non-negotiable constraints.

# Output contract

- One-sentence negotiation objective.
- BATNA action plan with evidence and confidence.
- Comparable offer-versus-BATNA table.
- Target package, opening position, and confidential reservation boundary.
- Issue-trade matrix and approved concessions.
- Decision log stating accept, counter, pause, or walk away.

# Metrics

- Value of signed package versus estimated BATNA.
- Percentage of material terms normalized before decision.
- BATNA execution readiness and time to activate.
- Concessions exchanged for reciprocal value.
- Post-deal implementation success and dispute rate.

# Failure modes

- Calling an attractive possibility a BATNA without making it executable.
- Comparing price while ignoring scope, risk, timing, or enforceability.
- Treating the reservation boundary as an opening demand.
- Bluffing about alternatives and damaging trust.
- Failing to update the BATNA as conditions change.
- Accepting because of deadline pressure rather than comparative value.

# Guardrails

- Never fabricate alternatives, authority, deadlines, or competing offers.
- Keep confidential limits within the authorized negotiation team.
- Obtain specialist review for regulated, contractual, employment, or investment terms.
- Do not use BATNA analysis to justify coercion, deception, or unlawful collusion.
- Compare full packages and document uncertainty explicitly.

# Adaptation notes

For small purchases, use a one-page comparison. For strategic partnerships, model
multiple scenarios and relationship effects. The source explains BATNA discipline;
the gates, roles, metrics, and output contract are operational adaptations for this OS.
