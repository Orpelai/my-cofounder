# Problem

Poor decisions often come from ambiguous accountability rather than weak analysis.
RAPID is Bain's role language for Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, and Decide.
The acronym is not ordered by sequence; it clarifies who does what around a decision.

# Trigger diagnostic

- Is there exactly one person who can bring the decision to closure?
- Is one owner responsible for producing a recommendation?
- Are veto or formal-agreement rights explicit and scarce?
- Is input limited to people with decision-relevant evidence?
- Does someone own execution after the choice is made?
- Can the decision deadline and criteria be stated in one paragraph?

# Required inputs

- Precisely framed decision and desired outcome.
- Options, constraints, deadline, and decision criteria.
- Stakeholder map and formal governance requirements.
- Evidence required before closure.
- Cost of delay and consequences of reversal.
- Implementation owner and capacity.

# Roles

- **R — Recommend:** develops options, analysis, and a clear recommendation.
- **A — Agree:** must formally agree where law, policy, or critical feasibility requires it.
- **P — Perform:** executes the decision once made.
- **I — Input:** supplies essential facts or expertise before the recommendation.
- **D — Decide:** one accountable person closes the decision and commits resources.

# Procedure with decision gates

1. Frame the decision as a choice tied to a specific objective.
2. Name one D before analysis expands.
3. Assign one R; split R only when the work can be cleanly integrated.
4. Limit A roles to genuine veto or mandatory approval rights.
5. Name only decision-critical I roles and set input deadlines.
6. Confirm P can execute and understands required capabilities.

**Gate 1 — role integrity:** stop if there is no D, more than one D, or an A role
that exists merely to preserve status. Resolve governance before proceeding.

7. R gathers evidence, tests options, and writes the recommendation.
8. I contributors provide bounded input; silence after the deadline is recorded.
9. A holders agree or escalate a specific objection with a proposed remedy.
10. D decides, records rationale, conditions, and reversibility.
11. P converts the decision into owners, milestones, and evidence of completion.

**Gate 2 — closure:** a decision is closed only when D records the choice and P accepts
the implementation handoff. Otherwise it remains open.

12. Review outcome at a predeclared date without reopening merely because someone disagreed.

# Output contract

- Decision statement, deadline, and success criteria.
- RAPID role map with named people.
- Recommendation with options, evidence, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Decision record signed off by D.
- Execution handoff with owner, milestones, and review date.

# Metrics

- Decision lead time from framing to closure.
- Percentage of critical decisions with exactly one D.
- Reopen rate absent new evidence.
- Input delivered before deadline.
- Time from decision to first execution milestone.
- Outcome quality against the stated success criteria.

# Failure modes

- Turning every stakeholder into an I.
- Giving multiple executives the D.
- Confusing A with broad consensus.
- Naming P after the decision without checking capacity.
- Using RAPID to legitimize a predetermined choice.
- Measuring speed while ignoring decision quality.

# Guardrails

- Formal legal, fiduciary, safety, or regulatory authority overrides the role map.
- D must consider relevant dissent and document material rejected evidence.
- A is not a courtesy title; use it only for real approval rights.
- RAPID does not remove the need for domain expertise or ethical review.
- Communicate the decision and rationale to affected implementers.

# Adaptation notes

Use a lightweight five-row table for recurring operating decisions and a full charter
for strategic choices. Bain uses RAPID as a decision-accountability tool; the gates,
handoff contract, and metrics here adapt it for auditable agent-assisted work.
