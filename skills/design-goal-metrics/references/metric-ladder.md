# Metric Ladder

## Outcome

Measure the final business or customer value promised by the goal. Use one metric that directly proves success; avoid an activity proxy.

Test: If this reaches target by the deadline, is the goal achieved?

## Leading indicators

Measure behaviors or states expected to precede the outcome. Choose two to five indicators close enough to update during the decision cycle.

Test: Would a change provide early evidence to continue, adapt, or stop?

## Inputs

Measure actions under the operator's direct control. Choose one to three high-leverage inputs tied to the leading indicators.

Test: Can the owner deliberately change this value this week?

## Guardrails

Protect trust, quality, safety, cash, delivery capacity, or long-term value from harmful optimization.

Test: What could improve the outcome metric while making the business worse?

## Causal check

Write the proposed chain:

`input → leading indicator → outcome`

State the evidence for each link. Treat an unsupported link as a hypothesis and design a test rather than presenting it as fact.
